# The Last Signal — story and threat redesign

## The premise

Mara Venn is not a stranger who happened to answer a distress call. She was Aster Station's acoustic-systems engineer: the person who calibrated its rescue beacon, emergency PA, and **Echo Lock** containment network. Five years before the game, Mara signed off on an experiment to map the flooded caverns beneath the station with a living resonance organism. When the experiment went wrong, the station was evacuated and buried the incident.

The automatic distress call uses the stored voice of Mara's former supervisor, Dr. Ilya Soren. Only Mara's voiceprint can restart the beacon and Echo Lock. That is why the signal called *her* back, and why she enters a station that should have stayed sealed.

## What the Mimic is

The Mimic is the escaped adult **Resonant**: a nearly blind cave organism adapted to map tunnels through vibration and reflected sound. Aster's team amplified that trait with the Echo Lock array and fed it archived staff speech to train it as a living survey instrument. It learned voices, announcements, pleas, and the cadence of the people who trapped it.

Its throat rings are not decoration. They are the biological resonators that create the copied voices and a visible echolocation pulse. Its long reverse-jointed limbs suit the flooded service tunnels. Its weak eyes are only vestigial amber sensors; it does not hunt by sight.

Mara's past decision gave the Resonant access to the station's acoustics, and the project taught it enough human sound to turn the crew's recordings into a lure. That personal responsibility connects her, the creature, and the station's equipment.

## Fair hunting rules

The Mimic obeys one consistent rule: it hunts **fresh vibration**, not an omniscient player marker.

- Walking creates a small audible trail; running, machinery, dropped metal, and crafting create a much larger one.
- It can only react when the latest sound falls within its hearing range. Walls, distance, and sound-damped rooms give the player space.
- During a chase it runs to the last loud sound it actually heard. Every additional loud footstep updates that target; stopping, doubling back, or using a quiet route lets the trail expire.
- When the trail expires, it searches the last sound source, then returns to stalking. It does not stop merely because an arbitrary distance counter ran out.
- Before an echo scan, the throat rings swell and pulse. A moving player in that pulse's range gives away a position; standing still or sheltering behind a closed acoustic barrier does not.
- The noise lure is an intentional use of the same rule: a speaker plays an overwhelming false trail and pulls the Mimic toward the shaft.

This makes the player learn a readable survival language: listen for the throat pulse, choose when to run, and make sound somewhere else when necessary.

## Supporting creatures: Echo Scavengers

The lower level contains **Echo Scavengers**, juvenile or parasitic animals that live in the Resonant's warm ventilation nests. They are not miniature copies of the main enemy and the Mimic does not magically summon them. They are quick, low to the ground, blind, and attracted to discarded batteries and exposed wiring.

They create a different kind of decision. In the current playable slice, two roam the lower wing; they can be avoided or temporarily stunned with a limited, noisy arc pulse. The intended full encounter design adds elevation and vent alternatives:

- A scavenger nest can be bypassed via a raised maintenance route or a crawlspace it cannot climb.
- A player can spend a limited arc charge / improvised maintenance tool to stun one long enough to pass.
- A short vault clears a low scavenger, but the landing makes enough noise to matter if the adult is nearby.
- Even if a later tool permits killing them, it must remain optional and never turn the game into a shooter.
- Nix, the tunnel rat, returns one additional arc charge after being fed; a safe vent shortcut is a later level-layout pass.

The adult Mimic remains the singular late-game hunter; scavengers establish the ecology and raise pressure before its full reveal.

## Revised playable arc

1. **Return:** Mara enters Aster after hearing Soren's recorded voice on the rescue channel. She finds a personal maintenance keycard and proof that the call is linked to Echo Lock.
2. **Restart:** Restoring the generator wakes the Echo Lock network and lets the Resonant use the station speakers again. The player first hears imitation before seeing the creature.
3. **Descent:** The freight elevator opens the pump level, where Mara finds evidence that her old test flooded the lower wing and released the organism.
4. **Exposure:** The player restores a pump, discovers scavenger nests and Nix's goshortcut, and learns the Resonant tracks vibration rather than light.
5. **Counter-signal:** Mara crafts a tuned noise lure from maintenance parts and uses the speaker system to pull the Mimic away from the final route.
6. **Beacon:** Her authorization starts the rescue beacon and re-engages Echo Lock. The final transmission contains the Resonant below, while leaving the moral question of what Aster did to it unresolved.

## Production implication

The creature model and level both need to show this fiction: animated throat pulses before a scan, a sound-dampened hide space, speaker routes, vocal logs from Soren, contamination/nest dressing in the lower level, and an animation set that changes from cautious listening to an animalistic sprint.
